# Assumptions

Every modeling choice that `SPEC.md` or a case doesn't settle is listed here with its default. "Toggle" means the engine will expose it as an option. "Fixed" means there is one behavior. IDs are stable; later milestones add to this file and never renumber.

Status:
- **Confirmed**: Jordan answered it on 2026-10-04, either before M1a was built (item N) or in his decisions on the M1a review (M1a review, item N). Items confirmed while M1b was built say (Jordan, M1b); those confirmed in his answers to the M1b review say (M1b review, item N). Items confirmed in his answers to the M2 plan say (Jordan, M2 plan). ## Rounds and cap tables

| ID | Question | Default | Toggle? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | "X% of the company immediately after the issuance" (Lena Fischer's 6%) | X% of all issued stock after the issuance. There were no options, pool, or SAFEs yet. x = p·N ÷ (1 − p), rounded down: 638,297 shares. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R2 | Pool created at X% of fully diluted shares | Fully diluted = issued stock + issued options + the pool. SAFEs are excluded, as the Millrace case states. Size = p·N ÷ (1 − p), rounded down. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R3 | Circular round math and share rounding | The price is solved exactly, treating new shares, SAFE shares, the pool target, and anti-dilution shares as fractional. Only the counts actually issued are then rounded down: each investor's shares, each SAFE's shares, and the pool, which is set to floor(target % × solved post-money FD) when there is a top-up (R16). The price stays the exact solved value, so the pool can land a fraction of a share under target. A holder with several investment lines in one round, say a pro-rata line and an ordinary one, gets one issuance: its lines are added up and rounded down once (edge case 18c). | Fixed | Default (item 4); one issuance per holder confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R4 | Post-money SAFE Company Capitalization | Follows the YC text. It counts all issued stock, issued options, promised options, the unissued pool as it stood before the financing, and the converting SAFEs themselves: each capped SAFE counts as purchase amount ÷ post-money cap of Company Capitalization, so CC = (stock + options + pool) ÷ (1 − Σ purchase ÷ cap). It excludes the pool increase made in the financing and the new money. | Fixed | Default (item 6) |
| R5 | The series a SAFE converts into | One series per distinct SAFE conversion price, named after the round's new series with "(from SAFEs)": Millrace's is "Seed Preferred (from SAFEs)". It was "(SAFE shadow)" until M4a; "shadow" is jargon founders won't know. Its id keeps the `_shadow` suffix, which cases' inputs use. Its original issue price and conversion price both equal the SAFE's exact conversion price. It copies the new series' preference multiple, participation, cap, and anti-dilution. Preference = shares × that price × multiple. For Millrace that is $674,999.64, slightly under $675,000 because shares round down. | Fixed | Default (item 7) |
| R6 | Pro-rata rights | NVCA definition. The entitlement is the investor's pre-round fully diluted percentage × the round size. The fully diluted base counts outstanding stock, outstanding options and outstanding convertible securities, all as converted, and leaves out the unissued pool. The entitlement is the most the investor may buy; the amount actually bought is an input. Millrace: Harbor Lane held 5,393,570 of 18,790,910 shares before Series A, or 28.703080%. That makes its entitlement $3,444,369.64, and its $3,000,000 is a partial take-up. Outstanding SAFEs and notes count too, as this definition says: each one converting in the round counts at the whole shares it receives there. In edge case 18, Investor Z's SAFE converts into 1,000,000 shares, so Investor X's share is 2,000,000 of 11,000,000, 18.181818%, not 20%. A pro-rata round with a SAFE that stays outstanding through it has no settled count, so it is refused. The total a holder marks as pro-rata in a round may not exceed its entitlement: otherwise the round is refused, with a message giving the entitlement, rounded down to the cent, as the amount to mark pro-rata, and the rest to enter as an ordinary investment in the same round (M4d). Buying more than pro-rata is common, as a super pro-rata or by taking up allocation others passed on (edge case 18c). In a pay-to-play round no investment may be marked pro-rata: the pay-to-play requirement takes the place of pro-rata there (R17), so the purchase is entered as an ordinary investment (M4g). | Toggle (`pro_rata_base_includes_unissued_pool` on the round): include the unissued pool, as it stood before the round, in the base. Millrace would give Harbor Lane 25.000000% and $3,000,000.00; edge case 18b tests it | Confirmed (M1a review, item 1); SAFEs and notes in the base confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning); the refusal confirmed (Jordan, M4a review); refused in a pay-to-play round (Jordan, M4f review) |
| R7 | Broad-based weighted average: what counts in A | NVCA: outstanding common, outstanding options as exercised, and outstanding preferred as converted. The unissued pool is excluded, including the increase made in the same round. | Toggle (`anti_dilution_include_unissued_pool_in_a` on the round, with the series naming the same definition, C10): include the unissued pool in A. Edge case 16e tests it | Confirmed (item 2) |
| R8 | Broad-based weighted average: B and C | B = the cash actually paid for the whole shares issued (shares issued × price) ÷ CP1. It is not the headline round size. C = the shares actually issued, after rounding down. That is how the charter computes it after closing. Millrace Series B: B uses $9,999,999.89 (9,241,189 shares × $1.082112), not $10,000,000. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1a review, item 5) |
| R9 | CP2 rounding | Exact, with no rounding. The NVCA model charter computes the adjusted conversion price to the nearest one-hundredth of a cent; under the toggle, the final CP2 is rounded to the nearest step, half up. The round's price is still solved exactly (R10). Edge case 16f rounds 16a's $2.1904762 to $2.1905. | Toggle (`anti_dilution_cp2_rounding` on the round): `exact` (default), `0.0001` or `0.01` | Default (item 3); the toggle confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R10 | Anti-dilution shares inside the round's own price | The post-money FD count used to solve the price includes the anti-dilution adjustment shares, computed from the solved (fractional) new-share count. The final CP2 uses the shares actually issued (R8), so the two differ by a fraction of a share. The new investors then get exactly money ÷ post-money, and the existing holders bear the whole adjustment (edge case 16a). Some deals price the new round without counting the anti-dilution shares; then the post-money fully diluted shares the price is set on leave them out, and the adjustment shares dilute the new investors too (edge case 16d). | Toggle (`anti_dilution_shares_in_post` on the round, default `true`): `false` prices the round without the anti-dilution shares | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R11 | Anti-dilution on Seed Preferred, Seed Preferred (from SAFEs), and Series B | None, because the case doesn't give any. It wouldn't matter for Millrace: the Seed price (about $0.4635) and the price of the series from SAFEs (about $0.3849) are both below the Series B price (about $1.0821), and nothing comes after Series B. | Fixed for Millrace | Default (item 13) |
| R12 | Seniority before Series B | The case states only the final order. After Series A, Series A is senior to the Seed tier, consistent with that final order. Exit values use the post–Series B table, so this doesn't change any expected payout. | Fixed for Millrace | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R13 | Who invests in Series B | Cobalt's $10,000,000 is the whole round. Harbor Lane doesn't exercise pro-rata. Ridgeline has no stated pro-rata rights. | Fixed for Millrace | Default (item 12) |
| R14 | Grants between rounds | They have no dates in the case, so `date` is null. All grants come out of the unissued pool. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R15 | Narrow-based weighted average: what counts in A | Outstanding preferred only, as converted. Common, options and the unissued pool are left out. Edge case 16's inputs name the definition each variant uses, in `anti_dilution_a` (C10). | Toggle: the definition of A | Confirmed (M1a review, item 2) |
| R16 | The unissued pool already meets the round's target | If the unissued pool before a round already meets or exceeds its target share of the post-money fully diluted shares, there is no top-up and the pool stays as it is; it never shrinks. The round is then priced on the actual pool, so the investors get exactly money ÷ post-money. Whether the target is met is judged on the post-money fully diluted shares without a top-up. Edge case 14b: the 1,000,000-share pool is 10.67% of 9,375,000, above a 10% target. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R17 | Pay-to-play: what a holder's pro-rata is | Each holder's share of the series' shares × the amount the round offers to that series' holders, which is an input. This follows the NVCA term sheet, where it is a share of the securities the board sets aside for existing investors. It is not R6's share of the whole company. Edge cases 17a and 17b: $1,000,000 offered to Series A, so Investor X (60% of Series A) must buy $600,000 and Investor W (40%) $400,000. **What counts toward it** (Jordan, 03d2 review): only the holder's new money in the round. A SAFE or note the holder converts in the same round doesn't count. Insiders often bridge before a recapitalization, and some deals count the bridge's conversion toward the requirement; that is on the later list as a possible toggle, with no case yet. | Fixed; the offered amount is an input | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R18 | Pay-to-play: what a holder who doesn't pay gets | Its preferred of that series converts to common at the input ratio, given as common shares per preferred share and rounded down (R3). It loses the preference and every other preferred right. Edge cases 17a and 17b use 1 common for 10 Series A. | The ratio is an input | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R19 | Pay-to-play: is the round priced before or after the conversion? | After. The conversion takes effect just before the round closes, so the round is priced on the cap table after it. The forfeited shares then benefit every remaining holder, and the new money gets exactly money ÷ post-money. Some deals set the price on the count before the conversion instead. Edge case 17a prices after the conversion and 17b before it. **With SAFEs or notes converting in the round (17i, the 0.3.0 plan's answer 4):** a SAFE's Company Capitalization and a note's base count the cap table the round is priced on, after the conversion by default and before it under the toggle, so the SAFE and note holders and the new investors see one share count. The engine follows since 03g; until then it refused it, as `pay_to_play_with_conversions`. | Toggle (`priced_after_conversion` in the round's `pay_to_play`, default `true`) | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R20 | Pay-to-play: a holder who buys only part of its pro-rata | By default, all of its preferred of the listed series converts, as if it had bought nothing (edge case 17c). Under the toggle, only the fraction it didn't buy converts: it keeps floor(shares × fraction bought) as preferred, and the rest converts at the series' ratio (17d). A holder in several listed series keeps the same fraction of each (17h). | Toggle (`partial_participation` in the round's `pay_to_play`): `convert_all` (default) or `convert_proportionally` | Confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R21 | Pay-to-play in a round that triggers anti-dilution | The conversion takes effect just before closing, and comes first. Holders who convert get the pay-to-play ratio and no anti-dilution adjustment; the preferred that remains gets the adjustment; and A counts the cap table after the conversion. This holds under both settings of R19: priced before the conversion, the round counts the converting holders' preferred in its price, but the adjustment shares in that price (R10) are still only those of the preferred that remains. Edge cases 17e (priced after) and 17f (priced before): Investor W's 800,000 Series A becomes 80,000 common unadjusted, Investor X's 1,200,000 are adjusted, and A is 7,780,000. **With a SAFE converting in the round** (17j, 0.3.0 work 03d2): the same, and the SAFE's conversion is a piece of the round (R25), so B and C count it alongside the new money; its Company Capitalization counts the cap table after the conversion (R19). In the engine since 03h. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R22 | Other pay-to-play choices | A holder takes its pro-rata if its total investment in the round is at least the required amount. A holder that invests nothing converts. Pay-to-play may name several series (edge cases 17g and 17h): the round's one offered amount is split across all their holders by their combined as-converted shares, so a holder in several series has one total requirement. Each series has its own conversion ratio, common shares per preferred share. The converted shares join the company's one common class. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1); several series confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R23 | Convertible notes converting in a priced round | A round converts outstanding notes only when it says so (`convert_notes`). Principal plus simple interest, Actual/365 to the round's date, converts (X3, X11), at the lower of the cap price and the discount price, the round price × (1 − discount); shares round down (R3). The pre-money cap divides by the share count just before the round, as at exit (X10's `conversion_base`: `with_pool`, `without_pool` or `common_only`), with the pool as it stood before this round's top-up and no SAFE or note converting in it. Edge cases 19a–19c, one per base; 19a's derivation shows what counting the top-up would give. Each note converts into a series of its own, one per conversion price, "Series A Preferred (from notes)" (id `<series>_notes`), with the new series' rights, priced at the note's conversion price, separate from the series from SAFEs. Refused, with a clear error: a post-money cap, compounding interest, and a minimum size for the round to count. | Toggle (`conversion_base`, per note) | Confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R24 | Pre-money SAFEs converting in a priced round | Follows the YC pre-money SAFE. It converts at the lower of the Safe Price, the valuation cap ÷ Company Capitalization, and the discount price. Company Capitalization counts the stock and options outstanding and the unissued pool **including any increase made in this financing**, and leaves out every SAFE and note. It converts into the series from SAFEs (R5). Edge case 20; edge case 21 converts one alongside a note, since neither counts the other. A SAFE has a `pre_money_cap` or a `post_money_cap`, never both. **A post-money SAFE converting alongside notes or pre-money SAFEs (21b, 21c; the 0.3.0 plan's answer 2):** its Company Capitalization counts their conversion shares, as YC's "Converting Securities", solved together with the round's price, since a note's or SAFE's shares at its discount depend on it. They still leave every SAFE and note out of their own bases. **The shares it counts are their exact conversion shares, before rounding down** (21d, Jordan's 03c review), the same way the round's price is solved exactly (R3): a note converting at its discount has shares that depend on the price, fractional until it is issued its whole shares. **A SAFE with no cap** converting beside a post-money SAFE is counted the same way, at its exact shares at the discount price: it is a Converting Security too. The reference always counted it; 0.2.0's engine built such a round without counting it (it refused only notes and pre-money SAFEs here), so 0.3.0 gives a different answer for it. The engine follows since 03g; until then it refused the rest, as `post_money_safe_with_pre_money_instruments`. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R25 | When anti-dilution applies, and to several series at once | A series is adjusted when the round's price is below its conversion price before the round; at exactly that price nothing changes. Several series adjusted in one round are adjusted independently, each by its own rule: A is counted on the cap table before the round, before any adjustment, and B and C come from the round's new shares (R8), so no series' adjustment enters another's A. A pro-rata entitlement in the same round (R6) counts holdings at the conversion ratios before the adjustment, since it is a pre-round percentage. No case adjusts two series; the engine matches the reference calculator on one in its tests (M4e). **With SAFEs and notes converting in the round (16g–16i, 0.3.0 work 03d; the 0.3.0 plan's answer 3):** a SAFE or note issued after a series is a convertible security whose share count first becomes known in this round, so its conversion is an issue of its own, tested at its own price, as the new money is tested at the round's price (3a, 3c). A series is adjusted when any piece of the round is priced below its conversion price. Only those pieces go into its B and C: B counts what was paid for each (the new money's cash for its whole shares, R8; a SAFE's purchase amount; a note's principal plus interest, the debt its shares cancel) and C their whole shares. A stays the shares outstanding before the round, and pieces at or above the conversion price stay out of the formula. A SAFE or note never converts above the round's price, so in a down round every piece counts (16g); in a round priced above a series, a discounted conversion below it adjusts the series on its own (16i). **Toggle** (`anti_dilution_exempts_conversions` on the round, under its "More terms"): the conversions are exempt, as by a charter carve-out or a waiver, and count in A at the shares they receive instead (3a, 3b; 16h). **A SAFE or note issued before a series** (3d; 16j, 03d2) was already outstanding when that series bought in, so its conversion doesn't count against that series: it stays out of B and C, and counts in A at the shares it receives, as an exempt conversion does. It still counts against any series issued after it. A note or SAFE can stay outstanding through a priced round (`convert_notes` or `convert_safes` off), which is how one issued before a series reaches a later round. **What a SAFE's Company Capitalization and a note's or pre-money SAFE's base count** (16g–16j, 17j): each series at its conversion ratio before the round's adjustment, since they are counted just before the financing and the adjustment comes from the financing. The reference has done this since 16g; it is written down in 03d2. **Recorded, not modeled:** testing the round as one issue at its average price (16i's derivation shows it leaves the Seed unadjusted there), and a charter that counts only the principal a note's company received as its consideration. **Still refused, as "later"** (Jordan, 03d review), and on the engine README's list of terms not settled yet: full-ratchet or narrow-based anti-dilution in a round with conversions (which piece's price a full ratchet takes, and whether a narrow-based A counts conversion shares, are unsettled); and a conversion counted in a series' A, exempt under the toggle or issued before the series, at its discount, in a round that adjusts that series, with the adjustment shares in the price (R10's default). There the price is the root of a quadratic, not exact. It is refused only where the series would actually be adjusted: in a round where no piece is priced below any series' conversion price, it builds (the reference's unit tests, 03d2). **In the engine since 03h:** it settles each piece by rule, as it settles a SAFE's cap or discount, and solves the round exactly. It refuses the two combinations above, as `anti_dilution_with_conversions` and `discounted_conversion_in_anti_dilution_a`, and reports each adjustment's pieces. Until 03h it refused every round with conversions that triggered anti-dilution, and built 16i under the rule above this paragraph, without adjusting the Seed: a changed answer, in the 0.3.0 release notes. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4e review) |
| R26 | R7's toggle in a round that tops up the pool | A counts the shares outstanding immediately before the new issue, so under the toggle it counts the unissued pool as it stood before the round, without the round's increase. No case tops up the pool with the toggle on: 16e's round has no top-up. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4e review) |
| R27 | The pool top-up in a pay-to-play round | The pool's target, and R16's test of whether the pool already meets it, are measured on the post-money fully diluted shares the round is priced on, so they follow R19: the table after the conversion by default, before it under the toggle. Priced before the conversion, the pool ends a little above its target share of the actual post-money count, since the converting preferred becomes fewer common shares. The other reading exists: "X% of post-money" read literally means the actual count after closing, which differs from the count the round is priced on when it is priced before the conversion. No case tops up the pool in a pay-to-play round; the reference calculator does the same. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4f review) |
| R28 | A round's seniority and the series its SAFEs and notes convert into | A priced round's `seniority` may leave out the series its SAFEs and notes convert into ("… (from SAFEs)", "… (from notes)"); each one left out then ranks alongside the round's new series, in its tier. How many of those series a round creates depends on its own numbers (two SAFEs converting at two prices make two), so an input written before the round is built, as an editor writes it, can't always name them. Seniority that lists them works as before, and every locked case lists them. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4j plan) |
| R29 | Warrants issued (edge case 22, M5c) | Warrants count like options everywhere: NVCA's "Option" includes warrants, and so does the YC SAFE's "Options". So every count that includes issued options includes them: a round's post-money fully diluted shares and its pool top-up target, a post-money or pre-money SAFE's Company Capitalization, a note's `with_pool` and `without_pool` bases, the pro-rata base, broad-based A, and at a sale the Liquidity Capitalization (X1, X14). A warrant for preferred counts as converted. They aren't drawn from the option pool. | R30 | Cumulative dividends on a round's series (edge case 23) | A priced round's series may carry cumulative dividends: `cumulative_dividend` with a `rate`, a `method` and `on_conversion`, and no `accrual_start`, since they **accrue from the round's date**, which the round must then have. ## Owed before release

Terms the engine refuses with a clear error today, but that are common enough that the release should support them. Each needs its case, worked out and re-derived, first.

- **A post-money SAFE converting alongside notes or pre-money SAFEs in one round (R24).** Common in real Series A rounds. A post-money SAFE's Company Capitalization counts every other converting security (R4), and their share counts depend on the round in turn, so the case must settle how the circularity is solved. Cases 21b and 21c settle it (0.3.0 work, 03c), and 21d (03c2) with a note at its discount, whose shares depend on the price: it counts their exact shares, solved together with the price; the engine follows since 03g. (Added in M4b.)
- **SAFEs or notes converting in a round that triggers anti-dilution (R25).** Common: a bridge SAFE converting in a down Series A when the Seed has broad-based protection. The case must settle whether the conversion is itself a dilutive issue under the charter, whether the converting shares count in A, B or C, and whether a SAFE converting below CP1 at a discount triggers the adjustment on its own. Cases 16g, 16h and 16i settle it (0.3.0 work, 03d): each piece is tested at its own price, with a toggle to exempt the conversions. 16j settles a SAFE or note issued before the series, and 17j the same round with pay-to-play (03d2). The engine follows since 03h. (Added in M4e.)
- **A pay-to-play round that also converts SAFEs or notes (R17–R22).** Common: insiders bridge with notes or SAFEs before a recapitalization with pay-to-play. The case must settle whether a SAFE's Company Capitalization, defined "immediately prior to" the financing, and a note's conversion base count the cap table before or after the pay-to-play conversion, which needn't follow R19's pricing choice. Case 17i settles it (0.3.0 work, 03c): they follow R19; the engine follows since 03g. Case 17j adds anti-dilution (03d2), which the engine follows since 03h. (Added in M4f.)
- **A SAFE or note with no cap alongside participating preferred with a cap (X9, X12).** A discount-only SAFE or note converts at a price it helps set, and with capped participating preferred sharing the residual, which series are at their caps depends on that price too. Cases 12i and 13h settle it (0.3.0 work, 03a): converting is still worth exactly its amount ÷ (1 − discount), taken out of the residual first, so payouts stay straight lines. The engine follows since 03e. (Added in M5b.)
- **A warrant for a series in a tier the carve-out shares, coming into the money on the curve (X17).** The warrant's new shares join the tier's claim, so its exercise is paid from the tier, and it can become worth it while the tier is short, where payouts curve. The starting point for its case: case 8's company with a flat 10% carve-out alongside the preferences. The warrant comes into the money at $1,052,631.58, where (X + $100,000) ÷ ($2,200,000 + 0.1X) = $0.50, with the tier still short. Payouts don't jump there; it's a kink on the curve. Case 8b settles it (0.3.0 work, 03b): the reference places the kink exactly, and the engine follows in 03f. (Added in M5g.)

## Later

Deferred by Jordan's decisions since the 0.3.0 plan, each until a case settles it. The engine README's "Not settled yet" list holds every term the engine refuses as `"later"`; this list also holds choices the engine makes one way today that could become toggles.

- **Full-ratchet or narrow-based anti-dilution in a round that converts SAFEs or notes (R25).** Refused, as `anti_dilution_with_conversions`. Which piece's price a full ratchet takes, and whether a narrow-based A counts conversion shares, are unsettled. (03d review.)
- **A conversion counted in a series' A at its discount, in a round that adjusts that series, with the adjustment shares in the price (R25).** Refused, as `discounted_conversion_in_anti_dilution_a`: the price would be the root of a quadratic, not exact. A conversion is counted in A when exempt under the round's toggle or issued before the series. Refused only where the series would actually be adjusted. (03d review.)
- **Conversions counting toward a pay-to-play requirement (R17).** Not refused: today only new money counts. A possible toggle. (03d2 review.)
- **The page's "Pro-rata counts the unissued pool" setting (R6),** under a round's "More terms": relabel it in plain words, as 03i did for the anti-dilution pool setting. For the 1.0 pass. (03i review.)

| X15 | Several unconverted notes at a sale (edge case 13g, M5b; in the engine since M5h) | Repayments rank equally: each is debt, ahead of all equity, and nothing in the inputs puts one behind another, so a shortfall is shared pro rata by repayment. | X16 | A SAFE or note that is indifferent, where its choice changes what others get (in the engine for SAFEs since M5g, and notes since M5h) | A SAFE takes its Conversion Amount, and a note converts, only when that strictly pays more. | X17 | Payouts that curve (case 10b, M5c) | `SPEC.md` defines a breakpoint as a slope change, and until now every payout was a straight line between breakpoints. A carve-out paid alongside preferences (X7) breaks that: while it shares a tier that isn't paid in full, it gets exit value × its claim ÷ (its claim + the preferences there), and its claim grows with the exit value, so payouts curve. On a curve the slope changes everywhere, so there a breakpoint is **where the formula changes**: one of the carve-out's tiers ends, or the tier it shares is paid in full. In 10b those are $10,000,000 and $11,052,631.58. `expected.json` flags each breakpoint with a curved stretch next to it (`payouts_curve_below`, `payouts_curve_above`) and gives the reason `payouts_curve`. The breakpoint is still exact: what changes there moves in straight lines (a tier edge is a fixed exit value; what is left for a tier less its claims is a straight line within one carve-out tier). On a curved stretch the reference checks that nothing which sets the formula changes, in place of checking that payouts lie on a line. `SPEC.md` says the same since M5d: a breakpoint is where any holder's payout formula changes, which on a straight stretch is a slope change, and curved stretches are flagged. **The engine since M5f:** on a curved stretch its finder (E18) extends only those two straight margins to place the next change, checks the stretch at 64 points instead of against a line, and reads payouts and slopes at the breakpoint itself. A decision changing on a curve stops it with a clear error, as it stops the reference. **Why that is rare** (M5f review): the curve exists only while the most senior tier is short, and then common gets nothing. So no conversion, and no exercise of an option or a warrant for common, can become worth it there. A series sharing the short tier would give up its share of it for a share of a residual that is nearly empty. **The exception:** a warrant for a series in that tier. Its new shares join the tier's claim, so it is paid from the tier, not from common, and its exercise can become worth it while the tier is short. Case 8's company with a 10% carve-out alongside the preferences shows it: the warrant comes into the money at about $1.05M, on the curve. Case 8b (0.3.0 work, 03b) is that company. There the warrant's gain from exercising is one straight line over another, (a·x + b) ÷ (c·x + 1), so the reference fits it exactly from three readings, checks the fit against a fourth, and solves where it is zero: $20,000,000 ÷ 19 = $1,052,631.58, where payouts meet, a kink on the curve with payouts curving on both sides. Any other decision changing on a curve, or a gain of another shape, or a jump there, still stops the reference with the guard error. **The engine since 03f** (Jordan's answer 7 to the 0.3.0 plan): checking a curved stretch at its 64 points, where it reads a decision change it gathers at least four readings still in the stretch's state (bisecting towards the change if there are fewer), fits the deciding holder's gain as (a·x + b) ÷ (c·x + 1) from the first, middle and last of them, and checks the fit against a fourth, to within 10^-20 of that reading. On 8b the fit is exact to 40 digits. Where the gain is zero, between the last reading in the stretch's state and the first one out of it, is the breakpoint, and exactly one decision must change there, with payouts meeting. Two decisions at once, a conversion group, a gain of another shape, a root outside that bracket, or a jump there stop it with the guard error, as they stop the reference. Two warrants for the Seed at different strikes give two kinks on one curve, placed the same way (a unit test, which the reference agrees with). **On the page since M5k3:** a chart draws a curved stretch through 64 points along it, the engine's own payouts, and the legend and hover readout read a value there from the engine, not from the line between two points, which on 10b can be $36.54 off. The "For you" line's rate on a curved side is the rate right at the breakpoint, read across a step of 10^-9 as the engine reads its slopes. **The "For you" wording, approved in the M5d review:** the rate just below and just above the breakpoint, "about" on a curved side, and one sentence on why the rate keeps changing. For Founder A in 10b: at $10M, "For you: just below here each extra $1M adds about $104,132; just above, about $79,339. The rate keeps changing on both sides, because the carve-out's claim grows with the exit value." At $11,052,631.58, "For you: each extra $1M now adds $742,500, up from about $84,286 just below. Below here the rate keeps changing, because the carve-out's claim grows with the exit value." **Curved above only** (no case has one yet; written in M5k3 and confirmed in its review): "For you: just above here each extra $1M adds about $X, up from $Y. Above here the rate keeps changing, because the carve-out's claim grows with the exit value.", with "up from" or "down from" by which way the rate moves, as in the below-only line. | Fixed | Confirmed (M5c review) |
| X18 | SAFEs beside a note at a sale (0.5.0, 05c) | Follows the YC post-money SAFE's liquidation priority and its Liquidity Capitalization. **Priority:** the note's repayment is debt, paid before the SAFE's Cash-Out Amount and every preference, as X12 already pays a note ahead of preferred. **The SAFE's Liquidity Capitalization** counts a converting note at its conversion shares, as one of the SAFE's Converting Securities, and leaves out a note being repaid, which takes payment in lieu of converting. **The note's base** doesn't count a converting SAFE: with and without pool count outstanding shares and options, and the unissued pool for with pool, not other converting securities. **Where each depends on the other,** they're solved together, as in a round (03g).
