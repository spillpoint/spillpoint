# Assumptions

Every modeling choice that `SPEC.md` or a case doesn't settle is listed here with its default. "Toggle" means the engine will expose it as an option. "Fixed" means there is one behavior. IDs are stable; later milestones add to this file and never renumber.

## Rounds and cap tables

| ID | Question | Default | Toggle? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | "X% of the company immediately after the issuance" (Lena Fischer's 6%) | X% of all issued stock after the issuance. There were no options, pool, or SAFEs yet. x = p·N ÷ (1 − p), rounded down: 638,297 shares. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R2 | Pool created at X% of fully diluted shares | Fully diluted = issued stock + issued options + the pool. SAFEs are excluded, as the Millrace case states. Size = p·N ÷ (1 − p), rounded down. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R3 | Circular round math and share rounding | The price is solved exactly, treating new shares, SAFE shares, the pool target, and anti-dilution shares as fractional. Only the counts actually issued are then rounded down: each investor's shares, each SAFE's shares, and the pool, which is set to floor(target % × solved post-money FD) when there is a top-up (R16). The price stays the exact solved value, so the pool can land a fraction of a share under target. A holder with several investment lines in one round, say a pro-rata line and an ordinary one, gets one issuance: its lines are added up and rounded down once (edge case 18c). | Fixed | Default (item 4); one issuance per holder confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R4 | Post-money SAFE Company Capitalization | Follows the YC text. It counts all issued stock, issued options, promised options, the unissued pool as it stood before the financing, and the converting SAFEs themselves: each capped SAFE counts as purchase amount ÷ post-money cap of Company Capitalization, so CC = (stock + options + pool) ÷ (1 − Σ purchase ÷ cap). It excludes the pool increase made in the financing and the new money. | Fixed | Default (item 6) |
| R5 | The series a SAFE converts into | One series per distinct SAFE conversion price, named after the round's new series with "(from SAFEs)": Millrace's is "Seed Preferred (from SAFEs)". It was "(SAFE shadow)" until M4a; "shadow" is jargon founders won't know. Its id keeps the `_shadow` suffix, which cases' inputs use. Its original issue price and conversion price both equal the SAFE's exact conversion price. It copies the new series' preference multiple, participation, cap, and anti-dilution. Preference = shares × that price × multiple. For Millrace that is $674,999.64, slightly under $675,000 because shares round down. | Fixed | Default (item 7) |
| R6 | Pro-rata rights | NVCA definition. The entitlement is the investor's pre-round fully diluted percentage × the round size. The fully diluted base counts outstanding stock, outstanding options and outstanding convertible securities, all as converted, and leaves out the unissued pool. The entitlement is the most the investor may buy; the amount actually bought is an input. Millrace: Harbor Lane held 5,393,570 of 18,790,910 shares before Series A, or 28.703080%. That makes its entitlement $3,444,369.64, and its $3,000,000 is a partial take-up. Outstanding SAFEs and notes count too, as this definition says: each one converting in the round counts at the whole shares it receives there. In edge case 18, Investor Z's SAFE converts into 1,000,000 shares, so Investor X's share is 2,000,000 of 11,000,000, 18.181818%, not 20%. A pro-rata round with a SAFE that stays outstanding through it has no settled count, so it is refused. The total a holder marks as pro-rata in a round may not exceed its entitlement: otherwise the round is refused, with a message giving the entitlement, rounded down to the cent, as the amount to mark pro-rata, and the rest to enter as an ordinary investment in the same round (M4d). Buying more than pro-rata is common, as a super pro-rata or by taking up allocation others passed on (edge case 18c). In a pay-to-play round no investment may be marked pro-rata: the pay-to-play requirement takes the place of pro-rata there (R17), so the purchase is entered as an ordinary investment (M4g). | Toggle (`pro_rata_base_includes_unissued_pool` on the round): include the unissued pool, as it stood before the round, in the base. Millrace would give Harbor Lane 25.000000% and $3,000,000.00; edge case 18b tests it | Confirmed (M1a review, item 1); SAFEs and notes in the base confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning); the refusal confirmed (Jordan, M4a review); refused in a pay-to-play round (Jordan, M4f review) |
| R7 | Broad-based weighted average: what counts in A | NVCA: outstanding common, outstanding options as exercised, and outstanding preferred as converted. The unissued pool is excluded, including the increase made in the same round. | Toggle (`anti_dilution_include_unissued_pool_in_a` on the round, with the series naming the same definition, C10): include the unissued pool in A. Edge case 16e tests it | Confirmed (item 2) |
| R8 | Broad-based weighted average: B and C | B = the cash actually paid for the whole shares issued (shares issued × price) ÷ CP1. It is not the headline round size. C = the shares actually issued, after rounding down. That is how the charter computes it after closing. Millrace Series B: B uses $9,999,999.89 (9,241,189 shares × $1.082112), not $10,000,000. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1a review, item 5) |
| R9 | CP2 rounding | Exact, with no rounding. The NVCA model charter computes the adjusted conversion price to the nearest one-hundredth of a cent; under the toggle, the final CP2 is rounded to the nearest step, half up. The round's price is still solved exactly (R10). Edge case 16f rounds 16a's $2.1904762 to $2.1905. | Toggle (`anti_dilution_cp2_rounding` on the round): `exact` (default), `0.0001` or `0.01` | Default (item 3); the toggle confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R10 | Anti-dilution shares inside the round's own price | The post-money FD count used to solve the price includes the anti-dilution adjustment shares, computed from the solved (fractional) new-share count. The final CP2 uses the shares actually issued (R8), so the two differ by a fraction of a share. The new investors then get exactly money ÷ post-money, and the existing holders bear the whole adjustment (edge case 16a). Some deals price the new round without counting the anti-dilution shares; then the post-money fully diluted shares the price is set on leave them out, and the adjustment shares dilute the new investors too (edge case 16d). | Toggle (`anti_dilution_shares_in_post` on the round, default `true`): `false` prices the round without the anti-dilution shares | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R11 | Anti-dilution on Seed Preferred, Seed Preferred (from SAFEs), and Series B | None, because the case doesn't give any. It wouldn't matter for Millrace: the Seed price (about $0.4635) and the price of the series from SAFEs (about $0.3849) are both below the Series B price (about $1.0821), and nothing comes after Series B. | Fixed for Millrace | Default (item 13) |
| R12 | Seniority before Series B | The case states only the final order. After Series A, Series A is senior to the Seed tier, consistent with that final order. Exit values use the post–Series B table, so this doesn't change any expected payout. | Fixed for Millrace | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R13 | Who invests in Series B | Cobalt's $10,000,000 is the whole round. Harbor Lane doesn't exercise pro-rata. Ridgeline has no stated pro-rata rights. | Fixed for Millrace | Default (item 12) |
| R14 | Grants between rounds | They have no dates in the case, so `date` is null. All grants come out of the unissued pool. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| R15 | Narrow-based weighted average: what counts in A | Outstanding preferred only, as converted. Common, options and the unissued pool are left out. Edge case 16's inputs name the definition each variant uses, in `anti_dilution_a` (C10). | Toggle: the definition of A | Confirmed (M1a review, item 2) |
| R16 | The unissued pool already meets the round's target | If the unissued pool before a round already meets or exceeds its target share of the post-money fully diluted shares, there is no top-up and the pool stays as it is; it never shrinks. The round is then priced on the actual pool, so the investors get exactly money ÷ post-money. Whether the target is met is judged on the post-money fully diluted shares without a top-up. Edge case 14b: the 1,000,000-share pool is 10.67% of 9,375,000, above a 10% target. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R17 | Pay-to-play: what a holder's pro-rata is | Each holder's share of the series' shares × the amount the round offers to that series' holders, which is an input. This follows the NVCA term sheet, where it is a share of the securities the board sets aside for existing investors. It is not R6's share of the whole company. Edge cases 17a and 17b: $1,000,000 offered to Series A, so Investor X (60% of Series A) must buy $600,000 and Investor W (40%) $400,000. | Fixed; the offered amount is an input | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R18 | Pay-to-play: what a holder who doesn't pay gets | Its preferred of that series converts to common at the input ratio, given as common shares per preferred share and rounded down (R3). It loses the preference and every other preferred right. Edge cases 17a and 17b use 1 common for 10 Series A. | The ratio is an input | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R19 | Pay-to-play: is the round priced before or after the conversion? | After. The conversion takes effect just before the round closes, so the round is priced on the cap table after it. The forfeited shares then benefit every remaining holder, and the new money gets exactly money ÷ post-money. Some deals set the price on the count before the conversion instead. Edge case 17a prices after the conversion and 17b before it. | Toggle (`priced_after_conversion` in the round's `pay_to_play`, default `true`) | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| R20 | Pay-to-play: a holder who buys only part of its pro-rata | By default, all of its preferred of the listed series converts, as if it had bought nothing (edge case 17c). Under the toggle, only the fraction it didn't buy converts: it keeps floor(shares × fraction bought) as preferred, and the rest converts at the series' ratio (17d). A holder in several listed series keeps the same fraction of each (17h). | Toggle (`partial_participation` in the round's `pay_to_play`): `convert_all` (default) or `convert_proportionally` | Confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R21 | Pay-to-play in a round that triggers anti-dilution | The conversion takes effect just before closing, and comes first. Holders who convert get the pay-to-play ratio and no anti-dilution adjustment; the preferred that remains gets the adjustment; and A counts the cap table after the conversion. This holds under both settings of R19: priced before the conversion, the round counts the converting holders' preferred in its price, but the adjustment shares in that price (R10) are still only those of the preferred that remains. Edge cases 17e (priced after) and 17f (priced before): Investor W's 800,000 Series A becomes 80,000 common unadjusted, Investor X's 1,200,000 are adjusted, and A is 7,780,000. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R22 | Other pay-to-play choices | A holder takes its pro-rata if its total investment in the round is at least the required amount. A holder that invests nothing converts. Pay-to-play may name several series (edge cases 17g and 17h): the round's one offered amount is split across all their holders by their combined as-converted shares, so a holder in several series has one total requirement. Each series has its own conversion ratio, common shares per preferred share. The converted shares join the company's one common class. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1); several series confirmed (Jordan, M4 planning) |
| R23 | Convertible notes converting in a priced round | A round converts outstanding notes only when it says so (`convert_notes`). Principal plus simple interest, Actual/365 to the round's date, converts (X3, X11), at the lower of the cap price and the discount price, the round price × (1 − discount); shares round down (R3). The pre-money cap divides by the share count just before the round, as at exit (X10's `conversion_base`: `with_pool`, `without_pool` or `common_only`), with the pool as it stood before this round's top-up and no SAFE or note converting in it. Edge cases 19a–19c, one per base; 19a's derivation shows what counting the top-up would give. Each note converts into a series of its own, one per conversion price, "Series A Preferred (from notes)" (id `<series>_notes`), with the new series' rights, priced at the note's conversion price, separate from the series from SAFEs. Refused, with a clear error: a post-money cap, compounding interest, and a minimum size for the round to count. | Toggle (`conversion_base`, per note) | Confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R24 | Pre-money SAFEs converting in a priced round | Follows the YC pre-money SAFE. It converts at the lower of the Safe Price, the valuation cap ÷ Company Capitalization, and the discount price. Company Capitalization counts the stock and options outstanding and the unissued pool **including any increase made in this financing**, and leaves out every SAFE and note. It converts into the series from SAFEs (R5). Edge case 20; edge case 21 converts one alongside a note, since neither counts the other. A SAFE has a `pre_money_cap` or a `post_money_cap`, never both. A post-money SAFE converting alongside notes or pre-money SAFEs is refused: see "Owed before release". | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4b) |
| R25 | When anti-dilution applies, and to several series at once | A series is adjusted when the round's price is below its conversion price before the round; at exactly that price nothing changes. Several series adjusted in one round are adjusted independently, each by its own rule: A is counted on the cap table before the round, before any adjustment, and B and C come from the round's new shares (R8), so no series' adjustment enters another's A. A pro-rata entitlement in the same round (R6) counts holdings at the conversion ratios before the adjustment, since it is a pre-round percentage. No case adjusts two series; the engine matches the reference calculator on one in its tests (M4e). | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4e review) |
| R26 | R7's toggle in a round that tops up the pool | A counts the shares outstanding immediately before the new issue, so under the toggle it counts the unissued pool as it stood before the round, without the round's increase. No case tops up the pool with the toggle on: 16e's round has no top-up. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4e review) |
| R27 | The pool top-up in a pay-to-play round | The pool's target, and R16's test of whether the pool already meets it, are measured on the post-money fully diluted shares the round is priced on, so they follow R19: the table after the conversion by default, before it under the toggle. Priced before the conversion, the pool ends a little above its target share of the actual post-money count, since the converting preferred becomes fewer common shares. The other reading exists: "X% of post-money" read literally means the actual count after closing, which differs from the count the round is priced on when it is priced before the conversion. No case tops up the pool in a pay-to-play round; the reference calculator does the same. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4f review) |

## Owed before release

Terms the engine refuses with a clear error today, but that are common enough that the release should support them. Each needs its case, worked out and re-derived, first.

- **A post-money SAFE converting alongside notes or pre-money SAFEs in one round (R24).** Common in real Series A rounds. A post-money SAFE's Company Capitalization counts every other converting security (R4), and their share counts depend on the round in turn, so the case must settle how the circularity is solved. (Added in M4b.)
- **SAFEs or notes converting in a round that triggers anti-dilution (R25).** Common: a bridge SAFE converting in a down Series A when the Seed has broad-based protection. The case must settle whether the conversion is itself a dilutive issue under the charter, whether the converting shares count in A, B or C, and whether a SAFE converting below CP1 at a discount triggers the adjustment on its own. (Added in M4e.)
- **A pay-to-play round that also converts SAFEs or notes (R17–R22).** Common: insiders bridge with notes or SAFEs before a recapitalization with pay-to-play. The case must settle whether a SAFE's Company Capitalization, defined "immediately prior to" the financing, and a note's conversion base count the cap table before or after the pay-to-play conversion, which needn't follow R19's pricing choice. (Added in M4f.)

## Exit waterfall

| ID | Question | Default | Toggle? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Vesting | Options and restricted stock are fully vested. | Fixed (`SPEC.md`) | Default (item 11) |
| E2 | As-converted shares at exit | Exact (fractional), with no rounding. This is economically equal to rounding down plus cash in lieu (see `SPEC.md`, Rounding). | Fixed | Confirmed (item 5) |
| E3 | Option payouts and the exit-value axis | The exit value is proceeds to equity before strike cash. Exercised options pay their strike, which is added to proceeds. An option holder's payout is reported net of strike, so the holder × security lines sum exactly to the exit value. | Fixed | Default (item 10) |
| E4 | Who decides to exercise | One decision per option strike price, since every option at a strike has the same economics. Each warrant decides for itself. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| E5 | Exits where converting or exercising pays exactly the same | When several stable decision sets pay every holder the same, the reported decisions are the ones with the fewest conversions and exercises: a series converts, or an option is exercised, only when that strictly pays more. Payouts are unaffected. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| E6 | Conversion decisions | Each series decides for itself. There is no forced class conversion in Millrace. | Toggle: series that must convert together (`SPEC.md`) | Default (item 8) |
| E7 | Capped participation after anti-dilution | Cap = cap multiple × original issue price × shares, and the preference counts toward it. Anti-dilution changes neither the cap nor the preference, only the as-converted shares used for participation and conversion. A cap below the series' own preference is refused with a clear error, since the cap counts the preference; a cap equal to the preference is allowed and behaves like non-participating (confirmed by Jordan, M2b review). | Fixed | Default (item 9) |
| E8 | More than one stable answer | Every distinct stable payout outcome is reported, and the point is flagged `multiple_equilibria`. A breakpoint is also logged wherever the set of outcomes changes. Millrace has none. **What the search before M2c found** (`reference/tools/stable_answers.py`, fixed seeds): in 3,600 random cap tables where every cap sits above its preference, no exit value out of 432,000 had more than one stable answer. Why, in the simplest setting: when a non-participating series converts, the common price moves to somewhere between its old value and that series' preference per as-converted share. So one series converting makes the others less inclined to convert, and series convert in order of their preference per share; two series can't each be the one that converts. That argument doesn't cover every mix of tiers, caps and groups, which is why the engine still checks (E15). The only two-answer case found combines a cap below its own preference with another cap equal to its preference, which no real charter has; neither alone produces it. The search also found exit values with no stable answer at all, every one involving a conversion group (see the M2b review). | Fixed (`SPEC.md`) | Default (item 14) |
| E9 | Reporting granularity | `expected.json` records payouts by holder × security, e.g. Harbor Lane's Seed and its Series A as separate lines. Holder and class totals are derived from those lines. Classes are: common, options by strike, each preferred series, and each series from SAFEs (R5). Converted preferred stays under its original class. | Fixed | Confirmed (item 15) |
| E10 | Breakpoint precision | Each breakpoint is given to the cent (half-up), with its exact fraction alongside. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| E11 | Series that must convert together: how the group decides | A class vote. Each holder votes for conversion only if it does strictly better converting than if the group stays preferred; a holder who is indifferent votes to stay. Votes are weighted by the holder's as-converted shares of the group's series. The group converts only if the yes votes meet the threshold. In edge case 6b, at more than 50%, Seed-2 holds a veto and the group converts above $30M. In edge case 6c, at least 50%, Seed-1 carries the vote alone above $10M. | The threshold is an input per group: `vote_threshold_percent` (default 50) and `vote_rule`, `more_than` (default) or `at_least`. The group itself is the `SPEC.md` toggle. | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| E12 | Warrant for preferred | Exercised warrant shares become shares of that series. They carry its per-share preference (the series' original issue price × multiple, not the strike), participation, cap, and conversion, and they convert with the series. The warrant is exercised when the shares it buys are worth more than the strike, and the strike cash is added to proceeds. Its payout is reported net of strike on its own holder × security line. | Fixed | Confirmed (M1b review, item 1) |
| E13 | Payouts that jump | `SPEC.md` defines a breakpoint as a slope change. A class vote (E11) makes some payouts jump instead. A jump is reported as a breakpoint flagged `payouts_jump`, located where the pivotal voting holder stops being indifferent: either where its two outcomes cross, or where they separate after being equal over a range (for example, a junior holder that gets nothing either way until the senior preferences are paid, then does better converting; sharpened in the M2c review). Because an indifferent holder votes to stay, the outcome from below still holds at exactly that exit value, and the new outcome applies just above it. In 6b at $30M, Seed-1 jumps from $1M to $3M and common drops from $26M to $24M. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| E14 | Engine precision and ties | The engine (M2 on) does all money and share math in decimal.js at 40 significant digits. An exact input such as `"2867562476/1309104599"` is divided out at that precision, so the engine agrees with the reference's exact fractions to far less than a cent. Two amounts that differ by less than $0.000000000001 count as equal, so at a breakpoint, where two choices pay exactly the same in exact math, the tie-break (E5) applies. Amounts are reported rounded half-up to the cent (E10). The engine keeps decimal.js rather than exact fractions because compounding dividends (X5, refused for now) will eventually need real powers. Engine tests compare payouts and breakpoints to the cent (`SPEC.md`, Rounding) and check at every point that the payout lines, net of strike, add up to the exit value and that holder and class totals equal the sums of their lines. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M2 plan) |
| E15 | How the engine finds every stable answer | The engine solves for the stable decisions directly, not by trying every combination as the reference does. Option classes are not decision-makers: their exercise follows the common price (E16). A conversion group decides first (E17). The series outside a group are solved from both ends: from "nobody converts", one at a time makes the switch that most improves its payout until no one wants to switch; then the same from "everyone converts". If the two ends reach the same answer, that is the answer. If they disagree and there are 12 or fewer decision-makers (series, conversion groups, and later warrants, SAFEs and notes), it checks every combination of their decisions; option exercise is not a separate decision-maker there, but follows the common price inside each combination. With more than 12, it reports the answers it found and flags that the list may be incomplete. If solving from either end cycles instead of settling, the engine stops with a clear error rather than looping. Before M2c, the reference is used to try to construct a case with two stable answers (see E8), and the result is reported. If two stable answers can exist under these rules, the engine closes the gap left when both ends agree by checking every combination once per segment of the curve, between consecutive breakpoints, since the set of answers can only change at a breakpoint. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M2 plan) |
| E16 | Option exercise follows the common price | An option class isn't a free decision-maker. Under each choice a series or a group weighs, the options settle on the exercise set where no class gains by switching (E5: not exercising on a tie). So a series compares converting and staying with option exercise re-settled under each, never with the options held fixed. Holding them fixed produced exit values with no stable answer; the two-answer search (E8) found that this rule gives the same answer as holding them fixed wherever that has one, and removes those gaps. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M2b review) |
| E17 | A conversion group decides first | For each of the group's two choices, converting or staying, every other series and the options settle around that choice. The group then votes (E11) on those two settled outcomes, and the answer is the settled outcome it chooses. This always gave exactly one answer in the two-answer search, the same as the earlier rule wherever that had one. If the other series settle more than one way under either choice, the vote has no single comparison and the engine stops with a clear error; the search never saw this. More than one conversion group is refused with a clear error until a case needs it, because the order in which groups decide isn't settled. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M2b review) |
| E18 | How the engine finds breakpoints (M2d) | It walks up from the bottom of the range rather than scanning a grid as the reference does. Just above each point it reads the answer and every margin that decides it: tiers not yet paid in full, caps not yet reached, the next option class's net if exercised, each series' gain from switching, each group voter's preference, and the same margins for every alternative those comparisons use. Between changes all of them move in straight lines, so the next change is where the first margin reaches zero; if a payout bends or jumps there, that is a breakpoint. Each stretch is checked at its midpoint, and a change the margins didn't predict stops the finder with an error rather than going unreported. Breakpoints are reported strictly inside the range (`SPEC.md`); the reference now applies this too, where it once reported one at exactly $0. The finder reads each stretch $0.0001 above its start (trying other small steps if the decisions are still settling), so two changes closer together than that are reported as one breakpoint, which keeps the reasons for both. If anywhere in the range there is more than one stable answer, the finder stops with an error: E8's breakpoint wherever the set of answers changes isn't built yet, and the two-answer search never found such a range. Reason codes match the reference's; the wording is the engine's own. | Fixed | Confirmed (M2d review) |
| E19 | Rounding share counts down at 40 digits (M4c) | A round's share counts are worked out from its exact terms (amounts, valuations, share counts), not from the rounded price, and rounded down (R3). A count that is whole in exact arithmetic can still come out a hair under at 40 digits (463,999.99… for 464,000), so a computed count within 10⁻²⁰ of a whole number is that whole number. No real count sits that close to a whole number without being one. | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M4c review) |

## Edge cases (applied in M1b)

| ID | Question | Default | Toggle? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | Unconverted post-money SAFE at a Liquidity Event (case 12) | Follows the YC SAFE. The SAFE gets the greater of its Cash-Out Amount (the purchase amount, paid ahead of common) or its Conversion Amount (paid alongside common on purchase amount ÷ Liquidity Price shares). Liquidity Price = post-money valuation cap ÷ Liquidity Capitalization. Liquidity Capitalization counts all issued stock as converted, all issued options whether or not they are in the money, and the SAFE's own conversion shares; it excludes the unissued pool. A SAFE taking its Cash-Out Amount is left out of the count, but then its Liquidity Price isn't used. The conversion shares are exact, not rounded (E2): the SAFE is paid cash and no shares are issued. Where both amounts pay the same, the case reports the Cash-Out Amount (E5). `DERIVATION.md` explains the terms in plain words rather than quoting the text. | Fixed | Confirmed (item 16; terms confirmed by Jordan, M1b) |
| X2 | Cumulative dividends (case 9) | Simple interest on the original issue price, Actual/365: the actual number of days from the accrual start to the exit date (leap days count), divided by 365. The exit date is an input in `inputs.json` (`exit.exit_date`). Edge case 9 runs 1,461 days, including 29 February 2024. | Rate type is a toggle (`SPEC.md`) | Default (item 17) |
| X3 | Convertible note at exit (cases 13a–13c) | Simple interest at Actual/365. The holder takes the greater of 2× (principal + interest) or conversion at the cap. Those terms are written into the inputs. | Terms are inputs | Default (item 18) |
| X4 | Preference multiple and accrued dividends | The multiple applies to the original issue price only. Accrued dividends are added on top at 1x, following the NVCA form of preference: Original Issue Price plus Accruing Dividends accrued but unpaid. With a 2x multiple, the preference would be 2 × OIP + dividends, not 2 × (OIP + dividends). | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X5 | Dividend options the reference doesn't model yet | Compounding dividends, and dividends that survive conversion (the `SPEC.md` toggle), are refused with a clear error rather than skipped. No M1 case uses them. A warrant exercised at exit gets no accrued dividends, since its shares weren't outstanding while they accrued. | Compounding and "paid on conversion" are toggles (`SPEC.md`) | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X6 | Tiered management carve-out | Tiers are marginal, like tax brackets: each tier's percentage applies only to the slice of exit value inside it, so the pool grows continuously and payout slopes bend at tier edges. Past the last tier's upper end, the pool stays at its maximum. A flat carve-out is one tier from $0 with no upper end. The alternative, where one rate applies to the whole exit value once it falls in a band, would make payouts jump at band edges. | Marginal tiers are the default; band-style tiers can be a later toggle | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X7 | What the carve-out is a percentage of, and how it's paid | A percentage of the exit value, before any option or warrant strike cash. It is paid before all preferences (the `SPEC.md` default) and split among the listed people by fixed percentages that add up to 100%. Each recipient gets a holder × carve-out line. A recipient who also holds equity gets both. "Paid alongside preferences" (the `SPEC.md` toggle) is refused with a clear error for now. | Timing is a toggle (`SPEC.md`) | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X8 | Escrow and earnout payments (case 11) | The waterfall runs on cumulative proceeds (`SPEC.md`). A holder's take from a payment is its cumulative payout after the payment minus before it. Conversion and exercise decisions are re-made at each cumulative amount, as if it had all been paid at closing. A strike is paid in the payment where the option is first exercised. Payments are nominal, with no discounting for time. If a payment is never made, the earlier takes stand. A negative take (a holder owing money back) would be reported as is, never hidden; no M1 case has one. Each take is rounded to the cent separately, so a holder's takes can differ from its cumulative payout by a cent. **For M5's payment view:** once earnouts are supported, if a later payment ever lowers a holder's running total (a negative take), the payment view shows a clear warning on that payment, not just the number (Jordan, M3 planning). | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X9 | Unconverted SAFE setups no M1 case uses | More than one unconverted SAFE, a SAFE with no valuation cap (X1's Liquidity Price needs one), a SAFE with a discount, and a SAFE alongside preferred stock (its Cash-Out Amount ranks on par with preferred, and with more than one seniority tier the YC text doesn't say where) are refused with a clear error rather than skipped. They are where the rest of the SAFE text matters, such as leaving cashed-out SAFEs out of each other's Liquidity Capitalization. Issued warrants would count in the Liquidity Capitalization the way options do; no M1 case has both. Before M5, add exit cases for a SAFE with a discount, a SAFE with no cap, and a SAFE alongside preferred, so every SAFE term the engine supports has a test. | Fixed for now | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X10 | Convertible note at exit: what the cap divides by (cases 13a–13c) | The cap is pre-money, so the note's shares sit on top of the count. Conversion price = cap ÷ (all issued common + all issued options, whether or not they are in the money + the unissued pool), counted just before the sale. The note itself is not counted. Notes have no standard form, so this is a toggle. Case 13a uses `with_pool`, 13b `without_pool` and 13c `common_only`. | Toggle (`conversion_base`): `with_pool` (default), `without_pool`, or `common_only` | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X11 | Accrued interest when a note converts at exit | Principal plus accrued interest converts: conversion shares = (principal + interest) ÷ conversion price, exact (E2). | Fixed | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |
| X12 | Other choices for a note at exit | Repayment is debt, paid ahead of all equity. So in a case with a note, the exit value is the proceeds available to the note and the equity together, and the repayment comes out of it (E3 calls the exit value proceeds to equity). The note converts into common. Interest runs from the issue date to the exit date; there is no maturity input. Where repayment and conversion pay the same, the case reports repayment (E5). Issued warrants would count in the `with_pool` and `without_pool` bases the way options do. Refused with a clear error rather than skipped: more than one note, a note with no cap, a discount, compounding interest, a post-money cap, and a note alongside preferred stock, a SAFE or a carve-out. No M1 case uses them. Before M5, add exit cases for the note setups the reference now refuses (a discount, no cap, alongside preferred), so every note term the engine supports has a test. | Fixed for now | Confirmed (Jordan, M1b) |

## Case file conventions

| ID | Question | Default |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Exact numbers in JSON | Strings: an integer, a terminating decimal, or `"a/b"` when the decimal repeats. `approx` fields are for reading only; tests use the exact fields. |
| C2 | Millrace's exit cap table | `inputs.json` builds Millrace from its events. Its `exit.cap_table_after_event` points at the post–Series B table in `expected.json` → `cap_tables`, which is the M2 exit input. In M4, building from the rounds must reproduce that table exactly. Since M4e the engine runs a case's exit on the table it builds from the case's own events. `readExit` still takes a table given by event, such as the one `expected.json` records, and the dashboard shows Millrace on that recorded table until M4i. |
| C3 | Edge case folders | `cases/edge-NN-<name>`, numbered as in `SPEC.md`'s list. A case with variants gets one folder per variant (`edge-05a-stacked`, `edge-05b-pari-passu`), each with its own inputs and identical except for the one changed term. A related case can join a number with the next letter: 06d (E13) and 17c–17h (pay-to-play) were added in M4a. |
| C4 | New cap table fields | `conversion_groups`: series that must convert together (E11). Each entry is either a bare list of series IDs, meaning more than 50%, or `{"series": [...], "vote_threshold_percent": "50", "vote_rule": "more_than" \| "at_least"}`. Warrant securities: `"kind": "warrant"`, a `strike`, and an `underlying`, which is `"common"` or a preferred series ID (E12). Confirmed (M1b review, item 1). |
| C5 | Dividend fields | On a preferred series: `"cumulative_dividend": {"rate": "0.08", "method": "simple", "accrual_start": "YYYY-MM-DD", "on_conversion": "forfeited"}`. On the exit: `"exit_date": "YYYY-MM-DD"`. `expected.json` reports the accrual under `exit.accrued_dividends`. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C6 | Carve-out field | On the cap table: `"carve_out": {"timing": "before_preferences", "tiers": [{"from": "0", "to": "10000000", "percent": "10"}, …], "allocation": [{"holder": "…", "percent": "60"}, …]}`. Tiers start at 0 and are contiguous, and the last tier's `to` may be null. Recipients must be listed holders, even with no equity. Payouts appear under security `carve_out`. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C7 | Payment schedule fields | On the exit: `"payment_schedules": [{"id": "…", "description": "…", "payments": [{"label": "closing", "amount": "…"}, {"label": "earnout", "amount": "…"}]}]`. `expected.json` reports each payment under `exit.payment_schedules`, with its cumulative amount, the decisions at that amount, and holder × security takes with holder and class totals. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C8 | Unconverted SAFE fields at exit | On the cap table: `"unconverted_safes": [{"id": "…", "holder": "…", "purchase_amount": "…", "post_money_cap": "…", "discount": "0"}]`, the same format the round cap tables use. Each SAFE is its own class: payouts appear under its `id` as the security, and its decision is `cash_out_amount` or `conversion_amount`. `expected.json` reports the Liquidity Capitalization, Liquidity Price and conversion shares under `exit.unconverted_safes`. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C9 | Unconverted note fields at exit | On the cap table: `"unconverted_notes": [{"id": "…", "holder": "…", "principal": "…", "interest_rate": "0.06", "interest_method": "simple", "issue_date": "YYYY-MM-DD", "valuation_cap": "…", "cap_type": "pre_money", "conversion_base": "with_pool", "discount": "0", "repayment_multiple": "2"}]`. On the exit: `"exit_date": "YYYY-MM-DD"`. Each note is its own class: payouts appear under its `id` as the security, and its decision is `repayment` or `converts`. `expected.json` reports the days, interest, repayment, conversion base, conversion price and conversion shares under `exit.unconverted_notes`. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C10 | Naming the anti-dilution definition | Optional, on a preferred series next to `anti_dilution`: `"anti_dilution_a"`, naming what A counts. `broad_based`: `"outstanding_common_options_preferred"` (R7), or `"outstanding_common_options_preferred_and_unissued_pool"`, which must match the round's `anti_dilution_include_unissued_pool_in_a` toggle. `narrow_based`: `"outstanding_preferred"` (R15). `full_ratchet` and `none`: `null`, since they have no A. A name that doesn't fit the method is refused. Cap tables in `expected.json` repeat the field when a series has it, and a round's `anti_dilution` details give A and B as `null` for a full ratchet. Confirmed (Jordan, M1b). |
| C11 | Pay-to-play fields | On a `priced_round` event: `"pay_to_play": {"series": ["series_a"], "offered_amount": "1000000", "conversion_ratio": "0.1", "priced_after_conversion": true}`. `conversion_ratio` is common shares per preferred share: a single number for one series, or an object with one per series, `{"seed": "0.2", "series_a": "0.1"}` (R22). `partial_participation` is `"convert_all"` (the default) or `"convert_proportionally"` (R20). The round's details in `expected.json` report, for each holder of the listed series: its as-converted shares and share of them, the required amount, what it invested, the fraction bought, whether it participates, and for each series its shares, how many it keeps, how many convert, and the common received. (M4a added the per-series report; 17a's and 17b's details gained it, and no value changed.) |
| C12 | How strictly the engine reads an exit input (M2a) | Unknown fields are errors, so a misspelt term can't be silently ignored. Summary fields meant for reading (`totals`, `approx`) are accepted and unused, as is `exit_date`, which only dividends and notes use. Terms that arrive later are refused with an error naming the milestone, checked before anything else: warrants, cumulative dividends, carve-outs, payment schedules, and SAFEs and notes at exit (M5). Round events were refused until M4; since M4e an exit names a cap table built from them (C2). A stated `conversion_ratio` must equal original issue price ÷ conversion price within one part in 10^30. A missing `conversion_price` means the original issue price, and a missing `unissued_pool` means 0. Each holder × security appears once, and share counts are whole. Confirmed (M2a review). |
| C13 | Saved files (M3) | When M3 adds saving and loading a cap table as a local JSON file, the file carries a format version field, so files saved by older versions keep loading as the format changes. Built in M3d as version 1: `{"format": "spillpoint", "version": 1, "name", "cap_table", "range", "view"}`. The cap table and range are engine input (C1–C4), with exact values kept for fields nobody edited. `view` is optional: the exit value and the holder you are. An unknown field or a newer version is refused with a plain reason. Each older version will be migrated forward once a version 2 exists (`apps/dashboard/src/file.ts`). Confirmed (M2a review); format confirmed in the M3d review. |
| C14 | Round fields for notes, pre-money SAFEs and the M4b toggles | A `notes` event: `"notes": [{"id", "holder", "principal", "interest_rate", "interest_method": "simple", "issue_date", "valuation_cap", "cap_type": "pre_money", "conversion_base", "discount", "repayment_multiple"}]`, as C9. On a SAFE: `pre_money_cap` in place of `post_money_cap`. On a `priced_round`: `convert_notes` (default false), `pro_rata_base_includes_unissued_pool` (R6), `anti_dilution_cp2_rounding` (R9). A holder may have several lines in `investments` (R3). The round's details in `expected.json` report `note_conversions`: for each note, its principal, interest, the amount converting, its base and base shares, cap or discount, the conversion price, shares and series; a pre-money SAFE's conversion also reports its Company Capitalization. Confirmed (Jordan, M4b). |
